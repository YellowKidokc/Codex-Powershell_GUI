# MALLEUS — Try to Destroy Your Own Position

> You are not playing devil's advocate. You are trying to kill the thing you built, with full force, and seeing what's left standing.

Devil's advocacy is theater. You make a polite objection, acknowledge it, and move on feeling balanced. MALLEUS is different. You construct the strongest possible attack — the version a serious opponent would actually use — and let it hit. If your position survives, it earned survival. If it doesn't, you just saved yourself from building on a corpse.

## 1. NAME THE TARGET

What are you attacking? No hedging. State it as strongly as its defenders would.

**This page's target:** The eight operators (AXIOMA, ISOMORPHOS, UMBRA, SIGNUM, PROVENIS, ATTRACTOR, MALLEUS, OPERATIO) are structurally complete, non-redundant, and universally applicable to any domain of human thought.

That's a massive claim. Now hit it.

## 2. THE STRONGEST ATTACK

Not the easiest attack. Not the most obvious. The one that would make a serious critic pause.

**Attack:** The eight operators are not universal. They are a projection of one specific mind's cognitive architecture — David Lowe's. He thinks in systems. He compresses instinctively. He looks for hidden structures. Of course a system designed by him, tested by him, and validated against examples he selected feels universal. But test it on someone who thinks narratively, emotionally, or associatively. A poet doesn't AXIOMA. A grief counselor doesn't MALLEUS. The "universality" is survivorship bias dressed in Latin.

That's a real attack. It's the one a serious epistemologist would throw. Don't flinch from it.

## 3. LET IT HIT

What happens when this attack lands?

**What survives:** The operators were not validated by one mind alone. Three independent AI systems with fundamentally different architectures converged on the same set. GPT specifically ran the "is anything redundant?" test and found no collapsible pairs. More importantly, the claim is not "everyone naturally thinks this way." The claim is "these are the structural operations underneath thinking, whether or not the thinker is aware of them." A poet DOES plant floors (AXIOMA) — they just call it "finding the emotional truth of the piece." A grief counselor DOES map shadows (UMBRA) — they just call it "identifying the underlying trauma." The operators describe the cognitive machinery, not the conscious experience.

**What takes damage:** The accessibility claim. "Anyone can open this vault and immediately think better" is untested on actual naive users. The structural claim survives the attack. The pedagogical claim does not — yet. It needs real-world testing.

## 5. WHAT THE ATTACK REVEALED

The MALLEUS didn't kill the system. But it exposed the real risk: **the system might be true and unusable at the same time.** Structural completeness doesn't guarantee that a real person, opening a vault for the first time, will know what to do. The recursive self-documentation (each page IS the template) is the current best attempt at solving this. Whether it's enough is an empirical question, not a logical one.

The next move is not more logic. It's a user test.
